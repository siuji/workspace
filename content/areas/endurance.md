Running is part of my life. It keeps my body and my head in place. It's my daily therapy.

More than 200 short races, between 5 and 10 km. Dozens of half marathons. 27 marathons and longer races since 2009, in 8 countries, including the 6 Abbott World Marathon Majors: Chicago, Berlin, New York, Boston, London and Tokyo. Boston twice, in 2016 and 2017, a race most runners can only enter with a qualifying time.

And here lives one of my biggest frustrations. My best marathon is 3:04:54, in Porto Alegre, 2014. I saw sub-3 pass very close, and it's a mark I never broke. It's one of those things you keep dreaming about. I still think one day I can get there.

Long distance teaches what no brief does. Plans change at kilometer 30, the body asks you to stop, the weather turns. You adapt, adjust the pace and keep going.

That is how I see work, too. Resilience is not about holding on to the plan. It's keeping the strength to adapt, to try something new and to keep growing, one kilometer at a time.
